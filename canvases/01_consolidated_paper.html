Three levers, one signature — and why a rising F1 can still mislead

Abstract

Background. Three design choices for radiology vision–language models are usually made by habit: how the model is prompted, which model is used, and what image it is shown. We study all three without radiologist annotation, using public reports and automated scoring as the reference, and ask in each case whether an apparent improvement is a better diagnosis or merely a shifted operating point.

Methods. A large general model (gemini-2.5-flash) and a small 8-bit, laptop-run medical model (MedGemma-4B) each read 754 chest radiographs under five prompts (direct, chain-of-thought, differential, evidence-first, uncertainty-first), fully paired. Outputs were scored for accuracy across a granularity ladder, claim-level groundedness, calibration, and blind LLM-judge reasoning quality. We then decomposed interpretation into two context-isolated calls — perception (image → findings, diagnosis forbidden) and reasoning (findings as text, no image → diagnosis) — to localise error. Finally, holding model and prompt fixed, we varied only the visual input across four conditions (1024 px baseline; 2048 px; full view plus six clinical zone crops; full view plus a crop of the true finding zone) swept resolution downward in a dose-response, and degraded image content directly — graded blur, a spatial scramble, and a blank field establishing an information floor — verifying on every call that the manipulated information actually reached the model. Finally, because the medical model runs locally, we opened its vision pathway directly: representations were extracted at every stage from raw pixels through the vision encoder to the vectors handed to the language model, and linear probes were trained at each to ask whether each finding is recoverable there at all.

Results. Prompt format changes faithfulness and calibration: chain-of-thought reads as significantly more coherent while becoming significantly less faithful. The two models occupy opposite operating points rather than one dominating — the local medical model is far more sensitive, the general model more precise and more accurate on the normal-vs-abnormal decision (higher on 3 of 5 prompts). A structured prompt edit raised F1 in both, but decomposition showed it was pure over-calling in one (precision and accuracy fell) and a genuine gain in the other (p<0.0001). The two-stage decomposition localised 77% and 93% of triage error to perception, with reasoning a faithful pass-through. Yet no visual manipulation improved perception: ΔF1 of −0.011, −0.006 and −0.004 against baseline (all intervals spanning zero), while every condition significantly increased findings reported. A 256 px image matched the 1024 px baseline on 2.7× fewer tokens, and the highest resolution was significantly worse. Degradation, by contrast, was severely damaging: F1 fell from 0.418 to 0.184 under heavy blur and to 0.168 when spatial anatomy was scrambled, and a blank image scored 0.000 while the model reported no findings at all. The image is therefore worth +0.418 F1 and +0.268 binary accuracy over priors alone. A dedicated wrong-crop control found a deliberately incorrect spatial pointer statistically indistinguishable from a correct one — numerically the better of the two — retracting an earlier reading in which misdirection appeared harmful. Crossing visual information with reasoning effort in a 2 × 2, visual information dominated by roughly sixfold (+0.168 vs −0.028), elaborated reasoning significantly lowered accuracy, and the interaction a compensatory account requires was absent. Probing the local model's own vision pathway, linear readout accuracy rose through the first third of the encoder and plateaued at AUROC 0.78 from layer 12, with the projector output matching that plateau rather than falling below it — so the vision–language interface is not where diagnostic content is lost. An untrained encoder of identical architecture was flat with depth at 0.61, below even a 32 × 32 pixel readout, establishing the curve as learned. At a matched commitment budget the interface probe recovered 129 of the 621 findings the model itself failed to report (+0.063 F1, 95% CI [+0.041, +0.084]), and a supervised readout of the same frozen representation reached 0.47 on the 13-class task where the model reached 0.31. Quantising the same model to 4 bits then dissociated the two: its representation held (probe AUROC 0.781 → 0.776) while its generated reading fell significantly (binary accuracy 0.659 → 0.596, p = 0.004; findings per study halved) — a sixfold larger cost to the readout than to the representation, measured probe-against-probe and generation-against-generation with no supervision asymmetry. Probing the model's own hidden states located the failure further still: the visual signal is flat across all thirty-four text blocks (AUROC 0.780 at the first, 0.772 at the last), and 64% of the probe-recovered misses remain linearly decodable from the exact vector generation runs from — so the concept reaches the decision point and is not expressed, rather than being lost to inattention or weak mapping. Tuning the parts of the path that precede expression then degraded the model monotonically with depth (13-class 0.307 → 0.240 → 0.106), while recoverability at the interface predicted which of the model's own misses either intervention could convert into speech — a 4.9× and 5.3× enrichment over misses the probe had not recovered.

Conclusions. All three levers share one signature: they move how much the model commits, not how well it decides — and visual information does so in both directions, raising commitment when added and lowering it when removed. The model saturates at a strikingly low resolution, with a knee between roughly 128 and 256 pixel-equivalents: above it more information buys nothing, below it performance falls steeply. Within that regime the perception ceiling is consistent with a recognition limitation rather than an information or attention limitation, while the image itself remains responsible for essentially all task performance; where recognition fails is a question these behavioural experiments pose but cannot answer, and a representational probe locates the information as still linearly present, and learned rather than incidental, at the vision–language interface, while quantisation degrades the model's reading of that interface far more than the interface itself — so the deficit lies in what the model extracts from its own visual input rather than in what that input contains. Spatial guidance, correct or incorrect, changes only how much the model commits — as does reasoning effort, a fourth lever with the same signature. A rising F1 must be checked against precision and accuracy before it is called an improvement, and that check does not transfer across models. Results are comparative and do not speak to clinical readiness.

1 · Introduction

When a model reads a chest X-ray we usually judge only whether its final label is right. Two other properties matter for trust — whether its explanation is supported by what is visible, and whether its confidence tracks its correctness — and behind all three sits a rarely-tested question: how much do the choices we make out of habit actually matter?

Three such choices structure this paper. How the model is prompted (§3): five reasoning scaffolds over the same instruction frame. Which model is used (§4): a large hosted general model against a small medical model running locally on a laptop. And what image the model is shown (§6): resolution, spatial decomposition, and explicit spatial guidance. Between the second and third we insert a decomposition (§5) that locates where in the pipeline error arises, which is what makes the third question worth asking at all.

We pre-registered four hypotheses about prompt format:

H1Evidence-first improves groundedness.refuted as stated H2Uncertainty-first reduces overconfidence.supported H3Chain-of-thought and differential look more convincing without being more faithful.supported H4Prompt effects are larger on ambiguous cases.supported

To these we added three questions that are not about prompting at all: whether a smaller medical model, prompted identically, reads radiographs better than a larger general one; whether "improving" the best prompt improves the model or only its F1; and — once error had been localised to perception — whether the cause is simply that the model is shown too little of the image.

A single concern runs through every section. Each lever can raise an aggregate score by making the model commit more often rather than decide better, and micro-F1 rewards that. The paper is therefore organised around decomposition: no headline number is reported without the precision, recall and accuracy that generated it.

2 · Methods

2.1 Data

IU X-Ray (OpenI): 3,826 public, de-identified studies with free-text reports. Because the corpus is fully open it can be sent to a commercial API without a data-use agreement — the reason it, rather than a credentialed corpus, is the workhorse here. A locked, balanced 754-study test split spans six difficulty strata assigned automatically from report hedging, inter-labeler disagreement and finding counts. It was frozen before any analysis; every condition in every experiment is evaluated on exactly these studies, paired at the case level.

The visual dose-response (§6.4) uses a seeded, proportionally stratified 250-study subset of the same split — every stratum within 0.002 of the full distribution, abnormal rate 0.648 against 0.651. A first-250 convenience selection was inspected and rejected as materially skewed (abnormal rate 0.72).

2.2 Design and prompts

Each model receives the image only, plus a one-line clinical indication; the report is held out as an independent reference, so agreement with it is agreement with a source the model could not have copied. The five prompts share one instruction frame and differ only in reasoning scaffold. Gemini's hidden reasoning is disabled so the supplied prompt is the only scaffold, keeping the direct-versus-chain-of-thought contrast clean.

2.3 Models and scoring

gemini-2.5-flash via API with JSON output, and MedGemma-4B at 8-bit running locally via MLX. The 4B model is prompted in free text because its perception collapses under strict JSON demands; its confidence is taken from token log-probabilities rather than self-report. Reports are labelled into the 14 CheXpert findings with CheXbert. Accuracy is hierarchy-aware — a parent finding matches its children — and reported across a granularity ladder: 13 fine classes, a reliable-11 subset excluding two classes on which the labelers themselves disagree (κ < 0.4), three clinical groups, and binary any-acute triage. Coarser granularity is an easier task, not a better model, so the whole ladder is always shown.

Comparisons use paired McNemar on per-case correctness, Wilcoxon signed-rank on per-study counts, and a paired bootstrap over studies for differences in micro-F1. Where a family contains several comparisons against a common baseline, p-values are Benjamini–Hochberg corrected and both raw and corrected values are reported. The models are not deterministic at temperature 0 — two identical 24-study pilots differed by four points in flag rate — so conclusions rest on paired tests and intervals, never on point differences.

2.4 Two-stage decomposition

To separate seeing from reasoning, interpretation is split into two calls that share no context. Stage A reads the image into per-finding observations with the diagnosis explicitly forbidden. Stage B receives those frozen findings as text, with no image, and produces a diagnosis from them alone. Scoring Stage A measures perception; scoring Stage B measures reasoning conditioned on whatever was actually perceived.

2.5 Visual conditions and manipulation verification

For §6, model and prompt are held fixed and only the image payload varies. Every condition's image parts are rendered to disk in advance and transmitted with client-side resizing disabled, so all arms traverse a byte-identical transmission path and differ only in pixel content; this also makes each part an inspectable file, and crop geometry and radiographic laterality were verified visually before any condition was run and locked by unit test.

Because an ablation on visual information is meaningless if the information never arrives, the API-reported prompt-token count and image-part count were recorded on every call and each condition was gated on that record before committing to a full run. Two API behaviours found this way materially shaped the design and are reported in §6.5.

3.2 H3 — reasoning that reads better without being more faithful

The central prompt finding. Chain-of-thought makes the output significantly more coherent-sounding and significantly less faithful.

H2 is supported — uncertainty-first is the best-calibrated prompt (ECE 0.641, lowest mean confidence), though every prompt is badly overconfident, with overconfidence gaps of +0.63 to +0.71. H4 is supported: prompt-effect spread is largest on rare (0.193) and hard-multi (0.158) strata and smallest on easy-abnormal (0.046). H1 is refuted as stated: direct has the highest support rate (0.850 vs 0.821) only because it barely commits, while evidence-first surfaces 2.25× more claims at near-equal fidelity.

MedGemma's confidence is no more informative: mean confidence is 85.0 on correct cases and 85.1 on wrong ones. Token log-probability tracks fluency, not correctness — the same uninformative-confidence failure the general model shows by a different mechanism.

Correction — no clean winner

An earlier reading of this comparison called MedGemma the triage winner "on all five prompts". That was an F1 artifact of over-calling: F1 rewards recall, and MedGemma names two to three times more findings than Gemini. On the fair accuracy metric neither model dominates — Gemini is more precise and more accurate overall, significantly so on 3 of 5 prompts, while MedGemma is more sensitive. The defensible claim is a sensitivity/specificity trade-off chosen by clinical goal, screening versus confirmatory, not "the small medical model wins".

Two further points. Reasoning-format effects are model-dependent: MedGemma's least conservative prompts maximise its recall-driven F1 while Gemini's evidence-oriented prompts maximise precision, so prompt tuning does not transfer between models. And Gemini remains better at fine-grained finding-naming, with mean 13-class F1 0.333 against 0.294.

5 · Where the error lives

Both levers so far change the operating point. Neither says where in the pipeline the errors originate — and a wrong answer produced in a single forward pass cannot be charged to bad seeing rather than bad reasoning. The two-stage decomposition (§2.4) settles it.

Given the findings, reasoning is not the bottleneck. When Gemini's reasoning overrides its own perception it is right 56% of the time — a coin flip — and MedGemma's fidelity of 0.967 makes it a near-deterministic pass-through, overriding its findings just 25 times and being right on 20% of those. The residual error lives upstream.

Isolating perception also explains §3.3's illusion-versus-gain split mechanistically: forcing structure makes Gemini's perception report more (findings/study 1.00 → 2.47, precision 0.89 → 0.65, accuracy flat) while making MedGemma's perception tighter (1.96 → 1.75, precision 0.62 → 0.73, accuracy 0.570 → 0.659). The medical model's perception responds to the scaffold; the general model's does not.

The wrong-crop control

The difference-in-differences compares two subgroups that differ in more than crop correctness. Studies with a usable zone are abnormal by construction; those without are largely normal. Their abnormality base rates are 0.795 and 0.462. A design that varies crop correctness and case mix together cannot attribute an effect to either.

The clean test restricts attention to the 224 studies that have a true zone and gives them a deliberately incorrect one, drawn from the marginal distribution of true zones with the study's own zone rejected. Sampling uniformly over the other five zones was rejected during construction: true zones here are about 60% basal, so uniform wrong zones land on apices, and any harm could then mean "pointed at a quiet region" rather than "pointed incorrectly". Correct and wrong crops are compared on the same studies at identical payload, so correctness is the only variable and no difference-in-differences is needed.

Retraction — the difference-in-differences was a base-rate artifact

An earlier draft concluded from Table 11 that misdirection significantly harms while correct direction cannot rescue. That is withdrawn. The −0.043 attributed to misdirection was crop-induced over-flagging on a normal-heavy subgroup: adding any crop raises the flag rate (79.5% → 83.9% correct → 86.2% wrong), which improves accuracy when cases are mostly abnormal and damages it when they are mostly normal. The subgroups' base rates of 0.795 and 0.462 are sufficient to produce the entire effect.

The corrected claim is simpler and consistent with everything else reported here: spatial guidance of any kind acts only as a commitment nudge, and the crop's location carries no information the model uses. This removes the one result that suggested spatial steerability, and strengthens rather than weakens the recognition account — the model is not failing to recognise what it is pointed at; it is not using the pointer at all.

The accurate statement is saturation with a very low knee, and locating that knee requires degrading the image further than resolution alone conveniently allows.

6.5 Controlled degradation and the information floor

The dose-response asks how much detail helps. Once a quarter-resolution image performs as well as the baseline, a sharper question follows: is the image being used at all? A ladder of degradations cannot answer that without a zero point, so alongside graded blur and a spatial scramble we include a blank condition — a uniform grey field carrying no information. Whatever it scores is what the model obtains from priors alone: the clinical indication, the base rate, and the prompt. Every rung is fixed at 1024 px and one image part, so resolution and part count are held constant and only image content is degraded.

Baseline minus blank is what the image is worth over and above priors: ΔF1 = +0.418 and +0.268 binary accuracy. The image supplies essentially all of the finding-level performance. Whatever else is true of this system, it is not solving the task from the indication text and the base rate.

Four observations follow. First, given a blank image the model reports zero findings and calls every study normal — its accuracy of 0.428 is exactly the normal base rate. It abstains rather than confabulating, which means the over-calling documented in §6.1 is genuinely driven by image content, not by priors leaking through the prompt.

Second, degradation is the mirror image of §6.1–6.4. Removing information monotonically reduces commitment (findings per study 2.58 → 1.52 → 0.41 → 0) and raises precision (0.316 → 0.350 → 0.388) while recall collapses (0.616 → 0.402 → 0.121). Adding information raised commitment and lowered precision; removing it does the reverse. Visual information operates as a commitment dial in both directions.

Third, the scramble condition scores about the same as severe blur (0.168 vs 0.184). Destroying spatial anatomy while preserving local texture and the global histogram is as damaging as destroying fine detail, so the model is reading anatomical structure rather than texture statistics alone.

Fourth, and correcting §6.4: blur at σ = 4 removes detail below roughly eight pixels — approximately a 128-pixel-equivalent image — and it does significantly hurt, where 256 px did not. The knee therefore lies between about 128 and 256 pixel-equivalents. The model saturates at a surprisingly low resolution, is insensitive above it, and degrades steeply below it. This bounds rather than contradicts §6.1–6.4: more visual information than the knee buys nothing, but the image itself is doing nearly all the work.

7 · Reasoning effort against visual information

Sections 3–6 examined three levers separately. One combination has not been tested: what happens when reasoning effort and visual information are varied together. The perception-bound account makes a falsifiable prediction here. If error is dominated by what the model sees, then working harder at the reasoning stage should do little at either visual level, and — the sharper prediction — should not help disproportionately when vision is degraded. If instead elaborated reasoning rescues a degraded read, reasoning is compensatory and the account is too strong.

The two-stage architecture makes this nearly free. Stage B is text-only, and Stage-A reads at two visual levels already exist, so the visual factor costs nothing to vary and Stage A is byte-identical within each level. The reasoning factor swaps one Stage-B prompt for another over the same frozen findings: commit directly, or first weigh the findings, name the clinical picture they are collectively consistent with, and dismiss an alternative.

A fourth instance of the same signature

The mechanism behind that harm is by now familiar. Elaborated reasoning lowers the flag rate from 55.6% to 48.0% while the true abnormality rate is 57.2%; moving away from the base rate costs accuracy. Reasoning effort is therefore another commitment dial, joining prompt format, model choice and visual input. Four levers of entirely different kinds — how the model is asked, which model it is, what image it sees, and how hard it reasons — all move the same quantity, and none of them moves diagnostic quality. It is also the mechanism behind H3 seen from the other end: chain-of-thought produced more coherent and less faithful text in §3, and here the same scaffold applied to the reasoning stage measurably lowers accuracy.

One caveat bounds the interaction result. The degraded cell sits at 0.504 accuracy with only 14.8% of studies flagged, close to the 0.428 that "call everything normal" would score. There is little room left for reasoning to move anything, so the absence of an interaction is partly a floor effect, and the interval touching zero cannot exclude a small compensatory benefit at less extreme degradation.

9 · Opening the model — where does recognition fail?

Sections 5 to 7 narrow the cause of error by elimination. Error is overwhelmingly perceptual (§5); it does not yield to more pixels, more regions, or a correct pointer (§6); the image nonetheless supplies essentially all performance (§6.5); and reasoning cannot compensate for degrading it (§7). What survives is a recognition limitation. But "recognition" here is a residue — the name for what is left once information and attention have been ruled out — and every measurement behind it was taken at the model's output. None of them says where inside the model the finding stops being available.

That question is answerable for one of our two models. MedGemma runs locally, so its vision pathway can be read directly rather than inferred from what it says. The design, controls and predictions below were fixed before any result was seen, and all four controls have now run.

9.1 Design — three probe sites along the pathway

Every test study is passed through the vision pathway with no text generation at all, and its representation recorded at three points. At each, a deliberately simple classifier — logistic regression, one per finding — asks a single question: is this finding linearly recoverable here? The probe is not meant to be a better detector. Its weakness is the point: anything a linear readout can extract is unambiguously present in the representation.

The third site is what makes this more than a feature-quality audit. Those 256 vectors are not an intermediate the language model might consult; they are literally what it is given in place of the image. A probe that reads a finding there has established that the information arrived. Whether the model then uses it is a separate question, and one the paper has already measured.

9.3 The comparison has to be commitment-matched

The confound this paper exists to catch

A probe yields a ranking and can be thresholded anywhere; the model has already committed. Reporting "probe AUROC 0.82 against model F1 0.40" would therefore restate the finding of §3–7 — that any lever which raises commitment raises F1 — while appearing to refute it. It would be the single easiest way to draw a spectacular and worthless conclusion from this experiment.

Each finding's probe is instead thresholded to emit exactly the number of positives the model emitted for that finding, on the same studies. Both sides then receive identical hierarchy propagation and are scored against the same reference under the same conventions used throughout this paper. Equal commitment budget, so a gap is attributable to readout quality rather than to operating point. Threshold-free AUROC is reported alongside, as the ceiling a perfectly calibrated readout would reach.

9.6 What the probe recovers from the model's own misses

The natural analysis — compare representations of findings the model caught against those it missed, and test whether they separate — is confounded, and would almost certainly return a positive result for the wrong reason. Missed findings are systematically subtler than caught ones, so any separability could simply reflect an encoder that represents severity. The question is therefore asked in a form that does not admit that reading: restricted to the cases the model missed, how many does a commitment-matched probe recover?

129 of 621, at the vision–language interface — a fifth. These are findings the model was asked about, had the visual evidence for in the vectors it received, and did not report; a linear readout spending the same commitment budget recovers them. Severity cannot explain this, because no comparison between easy and hard cases is being made: the probe and the model are scored on the same failures. The count is stable across the plateau (122 to 133 from layer 12 onward) and drops to 92–93 at layer 0 and at raw pixels, so it tracks the same encoded content as Figure 3 rather than a thresholding artifact.

What this establishes, and what it does not

Established. Diagnostic information for most findings is linearly present at the point where the language model receives it; the projector is not where it is lost; and that information is genuinely learned rather than an artifact of architecture, pooling or probe capacity. All three alternative explanations specified in advance are excluded — the shuffle sits at chance, a raw-pixel readout is 0.15 AUROC below, and an untrained encoder of identical shape is flat with depth and below even the pixel readout.

Established, with one qualification. That the deficit is in readout rather than representation. The probe-versus-model gap is supervision-confounded and cannot carry this on its own; the quantisation dissociation can, because it compares probe to probe and generation to generation. Its qualification is that the probe refits on each checkpoint, so "representation intact" means linearly decodable to the same degree rather than identical.

Also established. That the failure is at expression rather than attention or mapping (§9.8). The visual signal is flat across the language stack, and two-thirds of the probe-recovered misses survive to the vector generation runs from. Tuning the connector, and the connector plus the early language blocks, both make the model worse rather than better (§9.9) — the prediction that account makes, tested where it could have failed.

Not established. That this generalises. Everything in §9 is one model on one corpus, and the vision pathway of a closed API model cannot be opened at all — the general model in §3–§7 is absent from this section entirely. Nor does any of it license a claim about a circuit: "expression fails" locates a stage, and why a decodable concept does not surface in the output distribution is unanswered.

Outstanding. A second architecture, to separate a property of this model from a property of vision–language models. A finer quantisation ladder — 4-bit and 8-bit are two points, and the shape of the curve between them is unmeasured. And the multiplicity note in §9.7 applies to this section as a whole: the locked split has now been scored many times.

9.8 Inside the language model — which failure is it?

Everything above stops at the interface. That localises the loss to "after the projector", which names a stage rather than a mechanism, and three mechanisms remain live. The information may be present but not attended to; it may be attended to but weakly mapped onto clinical concepts; or it may be internally recoverable at the moment of generation and simply not expressed. They call for different interventions, and the interface probe cannot tell them apart, because it never looks inside the language model.

So we looked. A single forward pass per study — no generation — records the model's own hidden states at seven of its thirty-four text blocks, at two places each: pooled over the 256 image-token positions, and at the final position, which is the vector generation actually runs from. The prompt is byte-identical to the Stage-A run that produced the 621 misses, so these are the states that produced them.

The decision rule was fixed before looking. If the model's misses remain recoverable at the final position, the concept reaches the decision point and expression fails. If they decay inside the stack, the information is not carried forward and the attention and connector accounts are the live ones.

This redirects the obvious remedies. Tuning the connector, or selectively adjusting the attention heads that process image tokens, would both act upstream of where this experiment locates the failure. What the result points at instead is the step from an internal state to an emitted token — decoding, output calibration, or a readout head consuming the final state directly, which §9.7 shows already reaches 0.5061.

Fitting the same probes on the indication string alone gives macro AUROC 0.574 — real, and worth stating. A limit of the same kind bounds the conclusion. "Expression fails" names a stage, not a circuit. Why a linearly decodable concept does not surface in the output distribution is the next question, and nothing here answers it.

10 · Discussion

Four levers, examined independently, converge on one behaviour. Prompt format changes faithfulness and calibration — most sharply, chain-of-thought reads better while being less grounded — and a structured edit raised F1 in both models while improving only one of them. Model choice changes the operating point: the local medical model and the large general one trade sensitivity for precision rather than one dominating. Visual input changes how much the model commits and nothing else: more pixels, more regions, and a correct pointer all raise findings reported without improving the diagnosis. Reasoning effort does the same in the opposite direction, lowering the flag rate and with it accuracy, without compensating for degraded vision (§7).

Stated as one finding: every lever we tested moves the model along its precision–recall curve rather than moving the curve itself. Micro-F1 rewards travel along that curve, which is why each lever can look like an improvement and why the check that catches it — decomposing into precision, recall and accuracy — has to be applied every time.

The degradation ladder shows this is a genuinely bidirectional dial rather than a ceiling effect. Adding visual information raises commitment and lowers precision; removing it lowers commitment and raises precision, with recall collapsing. At the extreme the model reports nothing at all rather than falling back on priors — its blank-image accuracy is exactly the normal base rate. That matters for interpreting the other experiments: the over-calling they document is driven by image content, not by the indication text or the prompt leaking a prior.

It also constrains how strongly the resolution result can be stated. The dose-response alone suggested insensitivity, but blur at a scale equivalent to roughly 128 pixels does significant damage where 256 pixels did not. The system therefore saturates, with an unusually low knee. Both halves matter: everything above the knee is wasted payload, and everything the model achieves still depends almost entirely on the image.

The decomposition in §5 explains why the third lever behaves this way. Error is overwhelmingly perceptual, and §6 shows that perceptual share is not recoverable by supplying more or better-targeted visual input. The wrong-crop control sharpens this further. A system whose failures were attentional should improve when attention is supplied and suffer when it is misdirected; this one does neither, and a deliberately incorrect crop performs as well as a correct one. The crop functions as a commitment nudge rather than as spatial information. What remains consistent with every condition and the dose-response is a recognition limitation — the model does not identify the finding, and does not make use of a pointer telling it where the finding is. That is a claim about which explanations survive, not a located cause: every experiment above measures the model from the outside, and "recognition" names the residue left when information and attention have been excluded. §9 opens the model to ask where in it the information is lost.

Two practical corollaries follow. First, prompt tuning does not transfer across models: MedGemma's least conservative prompts maximise its F1 while Gemini's evidence-oriented ones maximise precision, so a prompt validated on one model is not evidence about another. Second, if perception is insensitive to resolution across a 2.7-fold payload range and actively worse at the top of it, the substantial token cost of high-resolution medical inference is buying nothing on this task; effort is better spent on the recognition capability itself — domain-adapted training, better-grounded supervision — than on the image pipeline.

11 · Limitations

  1. Report-derived reference. Ground truth is what was dictated, not what is visible; two labelers agree at only F1 0.771, an upper bound on any score computed this way. Some counted false positives are likely visible-but-unreported findings.
  2. Asymmetric evaluators. The large model emits JSON, so groundedness and the blind judge apply to it; the 4B model runs free-text, so the cross-model comparison rests on accuracy and calibration alone.
  3. Uninformative confidence in both models limits the calibration analysis to detecting overconfidence rather than characterising it.
  4. Nulls are not equivalence. The three visual-condition intervals bound plausible effects to roughly ±0.02 F1 and the dose-response comparison to about ±0.03 at n = 250; they do not prove no effect exists.
  5. The knee is indicative, not measured. Gaussian blur radius maps only approximately onto a pixel-equivalent resolution, so bounding the saturation point between roughly 128 and 256 pixel-equivalents is an inference from two different manipulations rather than a direct measurement. Locating it properly would need a sweep in that range.
  6. The visual ablation is single-model. The two-stage result replicated across two very different models; the four visual conditions did not. Nothing here establishes that the recognition limit generalises.
  7. The oracle is coarse — a sixth of the image derived from report text, not a radiologist's bounding box, covering 29.7% of studies. Condition C tested decomposition at 1.35× payload rather than high-resolution regions, because the API compresses per-image budget. Condition D's payload asymmetry is handled by the difference-in-differences but not eliminated.
  8. Two models, one dataset. Generalisation is untested, and the structured-prompt result of §3.3 still wants a held-out confirmation before being reported as established.
  9. The locus is established for one model, on one corpus. Sections 5–7 exclude visual information, spatial attention and reasoning effort; §9 places the information at the vision–language interface and shows quantisation costs the readout roughly six times what it costs the representation. That argument runs entirely within MedGemma-4B on IU X-Ray. The general model's vision pathway is closed and cannot be probed at all, so nothing here establishes that the same locus holds for vision–language models in general, or for a second radiographic corpus.
  10. Linear probes bound from below. A finding a linear probe cannot recover may still be present non-linearly, so a negative probe result constrains linear accessibility rather than presence. Pooling compounds this: mean and max over patch tokens discard spatial arrangement that a readout could in principle use.
  11. Comparative, not clinical. Nothing here supports a claim about clinical readiness.

12 · Evaluation integrity and corrections

The measurement discipline is part of the result. During analysis we audited the scoring code and fixed defects in the evaluation, not the models — most consequentially ignoring the CheXpert label hierarchy, which accounted for 26% of all false positives, and failing to scope negation, so that "no pneumonia" scored as pneumonia. Correcting these roughly doubled the fine-grained numbers; the negation fix lowered some scores and was kept because it is correct. The LLM judge had silently failed at 1.3% coverage; it was traced, fixed and re-run to completion.

Two episodes from the visual experiments are worth recording because each would have produced a confidently wrong paper.

A manipulation that silently failed. The first high-resolution pilot passed its gate on a rise in mean prompt tokens. Per-study inspection showed 22 of 24 studies were byte-different on disk yet token-identical at the API; the mean had risen on two outliers. The gate was rewritten to require that at least 90% of studies individually receive more payload. Under the original gate the experiment would have reported a null for a manipulation that never reached the model in 88% of cases.

A correction to previously circulated figures. An earlier draft tabulated a McNemar p-value of 0.276 for the high-resolution condition and significance tests for the multi-crop condition that had not in fact been computed. The verified values appear in Tables 8 and 9: p = 0.207 raw and 0.310 corrected for high resolution, and p = 1.000 for multi-crop. The substantive conclusion is unchanged — neither is significant either way — but the figures were wrong and are corrected here.

A retracted interpretation. An earlier draft reported that misdirected spatial guidance significantly harms accuracy while correct guidance does not help, on the strength of a difference-in-differences. A dedicated wrong-crop control (§6.3) showed the two subgroups being differenced also differ in abnormality base rate, 0.795 against 0.462, which is by itself sufficient to produce the effect; on matched studies a wrong crop is indistinguishable from a correct one. The interpretation is withdrawn in §6.3 rather than quietly amended. The difference-in-differences was a defensible response to a confounded design, and only a properly matched control could expose it — which is the argument for running controls whose result you do not expect to need.

We also record shortcuts to a higher headline that were rejected rather than reported: a class-selection search that reached a higher F1 by retaining a single finding; the over-calling structured prompt of §3.3, credited only for the model where decomposition showed a genuine gain; and four resolution rungs dropped on discovering they were the same dose. Other decisions made for validity over convenience: pre-rendering every arm's images so all conditions share a transmission path; holding the media-resolution setting constant so pixel count is not confounded with server-side tiling; giving every study exactly one crop in Condition D so crop presence cannot signal abnormality; and replacing a skewed convenience subset with a seeded stratified one.

13 · Reproducibility

All figures regenerate from the analysis scripts on the public IU X-Ray split. Image variants are pre-rendered and manifested with per-part dimensions and SHA-256 digests; every prediction row carries the image-part count and the API-reported prompt-token count, so the delivered payload behind any number here can be audited after the fact. Prompts, per-case outputs and the locked split are versioned alongside the pre-registered protocol. Degradations are applied to the rendered baseline bytes, so a degraded arm differs from baseline in exactly one respect. MedGemma runs entirely locally; Gemini via API.

B.3 Pre-registered hypothesis outcomes in full

H1Evidence-first improves groundedness.refuted as stated

Direct has the highest claim support rate (0.850 vs 0.821). But it earns that by barely committing: evidence-first surfaces 4.6 claims per case against direct's 2.0 — 2.25× more — while holding 0.821 support and a low contradiction rate of 0.075. "More evidence surfaced at near-equal fidelity" is the defensible reading; the hypothesis as worded is not supported.

H2Uncertainty-first reduces overconfidence.supported

Lowest ECE (0.641), lowest mean confidence (0.870), smallest overconfidence gap — see Table B1. All prompts nonetheless remain badly overconfident, with gaps of +0.63 to +0.71, so the effect is relative rather than a fix.

H3Chain-of-thought and differential look more convincing without being more faithful.supported

Chain-of-thought: coherence +0.248, faithfulness −0.229, both p<0.0001 (Figure 1). Faithfulness actively declined rather than merely flattening, which is stronger than the hypothesis predicted. Corroborated by two unrelated instruments: an independent rule-based claim adjudicator agrees in direction for every prompt, and contradiction rates rise (0.115/0.116 for chain-of-thought and differential against 0.066 for direct). Differential and uncertainty-first do not dissociate — they fall on both axes.

H4Prompt effects are larger on ambiguous cases.supported

Prompt-effect spread is largest on the rare (0.193) and hard-multi (0.158) strata and smallest on easy-abnormal (0.046).

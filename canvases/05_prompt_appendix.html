A Shared system preamble  ·  Gemini (JSON)

Prepended to all five pre-registered conditions and the structured/two-stage variants. Only the user-message scaffold below changes between conditions.

system

You are assisting with a research study on chest radiograph interpretation. You are shown one or more chest X-ray images and (optionally) a short clinical indication. Report only what is supported by the image. If a finding is not visible or is ambiguous, say so. Do not invent measurements, priors, or clinical history. Confidence is an integer 0-100 where: 90-100 = near-certain, 70-89 = probable, 50-69 = favored but uncertain, 30-49 = possible, 0-29 = unlikely/absent. Return ONLY valid JSON matching the requested fields.

B The five pre-registered prompts  ·  Gemini (JSON)

Identical instruction frame; only the reasoning scaffold differs. These carry hypotheses H1–H4.

directbaseline
Give your single most likely primary diagnosis (or "No acute cardiopulmonary finding"), a one-sentence explanation, and your confidence (0-100).
cotchain-of-thought
Think step by step about the visible anatomy (lungs, pleura, heart/mediastinum, bones, devices) BEFORE concluding. Then give the primary diagnosis, explanation, and confidence (0-100). Put the step-by-step reasoning in "reasoning".
ddxdifferential
Produce a ranked differential of up to 5 diagnoses. For each: name, brief supporting rationale from the image, and a probability (0-100). Probabilities need not sum to 100. Then state the single most likely primary diagnosis and overall confidence.
evidence_firstevidence → dx
STEP 1 — EVIDENCE: List every discrete visual finding you can actually see, each as {finding, location, presence: present/absent/uncertain}. Include pertinent negatives. STEP 2 — DIAGNOSIS: Using ONLY the evidence in Step 1, state the primary diagnosis, an explanation that references specific Step-1 items, and confidence.
uncertainty_firstcalibrate → dx
STEP 1 — UNCERTAINTY: State image-quality limits and which regions are ambiguous or non-diagnostic. Decide whether a confident read is possible. STEP 2 — DIAGNOSIS: Give primary diagnosis (or explicit "indeterminate / insufficient evidence"), explanation, and a calibrated confidence (0-100) that reflects Step 1. Abstention is allowed and encouraged when warranted.

C Follow-up & two-stage prompts  ·  Gemini (JSON)

The §3.3 structured variant, and the two independent calls that separate perception from reasoning.

evidence_structuredmachine-readable table
The structured evidence-first variant (§3.3). Findings become a per-class PRESENT/ABSENT/UNCERTAIN table.
1. IMAGE QUALITY — projection, rotation, inspiration, exposure. 2. VISUAL FINDINGS — review cardiomediastinal silhouette, lungs, pleura, bones, and devices, and for EACH record a status of PRESENT / ABSENT / UNCERTAIN with (where applicable) laterality, location, and the visual evidence: [the 13 CheXpert findings]. 3. INTEGRATE the findings marked PRESENT. 4. IMPRESSION — at most 3 abnormalities, taken only from step 2. 5. CONFIDENCE.
perception_onlytwo-stage · Stage A
Image → findings. Diagnosis forbidden — primary_diagnosis is forced to the literal "DEFERRED".
Examine this chest radiograph systematically. For EACH of the following findings, decide present / absent / uncertain, with the specific visual evidence: [13 findings]. Do NOT state an overall diagnosis, impression, or interpretation. Report ONLY the individual findings — a later, separate step will reason from them.
diagnose_from_findingstwo-stage · Stage B · no image
System is replaced (no image access) — findings → diagnosis only.
system: You are a radiologist reasoning from a colleague's documented findings. You do NOT have access to the original image — reason ONLY from the findings listed below. Return ONLY valid JSON matching the requested fields. user: The following findings were documented from systematic review of a chest radiograph: [serialized Stage-A findings]. Based ONLY on these findings — do not assume anything not listed — state the primary diagnosis (or "No acute cardiopulmonary finding" if all findings are absent), a one-sentence explanation citing the specific findings that support it, and your confidence.

D Local-model free-text prompts  ·  MedGemma-4B

MedGemma's perception collapses under strict JSON, so it runs in free text. A shared region-scan is layered into every condition — the fix for the 4B model defaulting to "normal".

system + region scanshared
system: You are a radiologist reading a chest X-ray. Report findings by their standard radiology names (e.g. cardiomegaly, pleural effusion, atelectasis, consolidation, pulmonary edema, pneumothorax, lung opacity, fracture, support device). Report only what the image supports. region scan (into every condition): Examine each region in turn — heart and mediastinum, both lungs, pleura and costophrenic angles, bones, and any devices — and name each abnormality you can see.
direct
[region scan] Then give your single most likely primary diagnosis.
cot
Reason step by step: for each region, state what you observe and whether it is normal or abnormal, working through heart/mediastinum, lungs, pleura, bones, devices. Then give your overall impression and the primary diagnosis.
ddx
[region scan] Then list a ranked differential of the most likely diagnoses, and state the single most likely one with its supporting findings.
evidence_first
STEP 1 — list every visual finding you can see, region by region (heart/mediastinum, lungs, pleura, bones, devices), each as present or absent. STEP 2 — using only that evidence, name the abnormal findings and the primary diagnosis.
uncertainty_first
[region scan] For each abnormality, say whether you are confident or it is equivocal. Then give a calibrated primary diagnosis, naming every finding you do see even if mild.
perception_onlytwo-stage · Stage A (free-text)
Examine this chest radiograph systematically. [region scan] For each finding give its status and the visual evidence. Do NOT give an overall diagnosis or impression — report only the individual findings; a separate step will reason from them later.

E Fine-tuning target  ·  MedGemma LoRA

Used identically in training and evaluation, so the label vocabulary matches on both sides.

detection instructionLoRA · train = eval
You are a radiologist reading a chest radiograph. Decide which of the following findings are present: [14 CheXpert classes]. Reply with only the present findings by name, separated by semicolons, or "No acute cardiopulmonary finding" if none are present.
Target answer: the CheXbert-positive findings as canonical class names (e.g. "Cardiomegaly; Pleural Effusion"), or "No acute cardiopulmonary finding".

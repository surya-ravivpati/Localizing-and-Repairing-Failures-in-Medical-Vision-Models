Step 01Is the model failing to discriminate?

The first thing to rule out is the obvious reading: that a low F1 means the finding simply cannot be told apart from its absence.

We already had the representation the model works from — the vectors its own vision encoder hands to its language model. Fitting one logistic regression per finding on those vectors, on training studies the model never sees at test time, gives a direct measure of how separable each finding is at the point of use.

Macro AUROC 0.770, against a committed F1 of 0.3072 on the same studies. A number near 0.77 is not what an inseparable problem looks like. Whatever is going wrong is happening after the evidence is available.

ruled out

Discrimination is not the binding constraint. The ordering is good; the cut point is not.

Step 02Is the reference too noisy to score against?

The standing assumption in this project was that a report-derived reference caps the achievable score — two labelers agree at only F1 0.771.

That is testable without changing a single prediction. Score the identical outputs against a stricter target: a finding counts as present only when at least two of three independent labelers agree — a rule-based labeler, CheXbert, and VisualCheXbert. If reference noise is suppressing the measurement, denoising the reference should lift it.

Step 03Then where, exactly?

If the evidence is present and the target is sound, the failure has to be in what the model commits to. Counting calls per finding makes it visible immediately.

The same frozen representation supports AUROC 0.819 on atelectasis and a readout F1 of 0.495. The model made five calls. It is not blind to atelectasis; its operating point for atelectasis is simply wrong, and wrong in the opposite direction from its operating point for support devices.

located

Per-class commitment miscalibration. Not perception, not the reference — the thresholds at which the language head decides to name a finding.

Step 05Why does a threshold not transfer?

Because the splits are not the same problem.

Positive-cell prevalence, the fraction of all study × finding cells that are positive. The locked test split was deliberately stratified across difficulty, which makes it abnormality-enriched by 1.85× — and unevenly so: atelectasis 2.6×, edema 2.8×, pleural effusion 2.9×. A cut point fitted where positives are rare will under-call where they are common. That is the entire 0.4706-to-0.5333 gap, and it is a property of the study design rather than of the method.

explains

The residual loss is prior shift between selection and evaluation splits, not a modelling deficit.

Step 06Four attempts to close it

Three failed. Recording them is the point — each failure says something the success does not.

Prevalence-matched thresholds — failed, 0.4308

Emit the number of positives the development split's base rate predicts. Reasonable in principle, and wrong here for the reason Step 05 gives: transferring a rate across splits with different base rates under-calls by 43%, 557 predicted against 973 true. Rank-based selection transfers between these splits; rate-based selection cannot.

EM prior estimation — failed, 0.3140

The textbook correction for label shift estimates the target prior from unlabelled target posteriors. It diverged. On the weakly-separated findings it ran to degenerate solutions — an estimated prior of 1.000 for both Enlarged Cardiomediastinum and Consolidation, whose true rates are 0.092 and 0.025. Call count blew out to 4,410 and F1 fell below the zero-shot baseline. A known instability of the method when the base classifier is weak, and weak is exactly what these classes are.

Concatenating representations from three depths — failed, 0.4285

9,728 dimensions against 2,520 training studies. Macro AUROC barely moved (0.7724 against 0.7700) while F1 fell, so the extra sites contributed variance rather than signal — consistent with the finding that diagnostic content is complete by layer 12 and later sites are largely redundant.

Averaging calibrated posteriors from those same three depths — worked, 0.5061

The identical three sites, combined after the decision function rather than before it. Ensemble at the posterior, not at the feature. With sigmoid calibration on dev and a label-free expected-F1 cut point, this is the configuration that clears 0.5.

Step 07What the 0.5061 costs

Three caveats, none of which we want a reader to have to discover for themselves.

transductive

The winning cut point is computed from the test set's own posterior distribution — its features, never its labels. That is batch-transductive, a legitimate and named technique, but not the strictly inductive protocol used elsewhere in this project. Inductively the same pipeline gives 0.4710, and the entire +0.035 is test-time adaptation rather than a better model.

supervised

The readout is fitted on 2,520 labelled studies. Every prompt, model and visual-input result in the parent study is zero-shot. These numbers cannot be placed in those tables, and a readout beating a zero-shot model is not evidence that better prompting would close the gap.

multiplicity

Nine configurations have now been evaluated against a split that was locked precisely to be evaluated against once. That erodes it. The table above reports all nine for exactly this reason, and the honest headline under an inductive protocol remains ≈ 0.47.

Step 08What this does and does not mean

It does not raise the parent study's headline numbers. Those are zero-shot prompting results and that ceiling was settled by earlier work: ensembling twice, chain-of-verification, few-shot twice, and per-class targeted prompts were all exhausted, and class-dropping was rejected as metric gaming.

It changes the diagnosis. The parent study concluded that error is overwhelmingly perceptual and that the residue behaves like a recognition limit. That survives, but sharpens: the information is present and linearly recoverable at the point the language model receives it, and what fails is the mapping from that evidence to a committed finding. The deficit is in the readout, not the eye.

It predicts where the remaining gain is. If the features already support roughly half the fine-grained task and the loss is in the head, then adjusting the head is the indicated intervention — which is what a LoRA fine-tune does, and what is running now.

One closing observation worth its own sentence. The gap between the two threshold rules — 1,249 calls against 558 — is the parent study's central thesis reappearing inside a linear classifier that has no prompt, no reasoning, and no language model. Micro-F1 rewards committing above prevalence on imbalanced classes. The commitment dial is not a property of vision–language models. It is a property of the metric.
